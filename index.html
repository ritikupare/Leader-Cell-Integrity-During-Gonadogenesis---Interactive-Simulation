What does this simulation model?

During C. elegans gonad morphogenesis, a single leader cell called the distal tip cell (DTC) migrates along a stereotyped path — first anteriorly, then making a U-turn dorsally — to shape the two arms of the gonad. The DTC's nucleus is always found at the migratory front. Agarwal et al. (2024) discovered that the KASH-domain protein UNC-83 links the nucleus to kinesin-1 motors moving on a polarised acentrosomal microtubule network, actively pulling the nucleus forward against frictional drag.

Critically, disrupting nuclear positioning alone (unc-83 RNAi) does not break gonad morphogenesis — actomyosin cortical contractility compensates by maintaining cell shape. Disrupting contractility alone (nmy-2 RNAi) is also tolerated because the nucleus stays at the front. But when both are lost simultaneously, the DTC stretches during the U-turn until it fragments into a nucleated cell and an enucleated cytoplast, causing gonad bifurcation.

Wild-type

Nucleus at front + cortical tension → normal migration, successful U-turn, single gonadal arm.

unc-83 RNAi (nuclear positioning ↓)

Nucleus drifts backward under friction, but actomyosin contractility holds cell together through the turn.

nmy-2 RNAi (actomyosin ↓)

Cortex is weakened, cell elongates slightly, but front-positioned nucleus prevents critical stretching.

Double knockdown

Nucleus lags + no cortical tension → cell stretches catastrophically at the U-turn → DTC fragments → gonad bifurcates.
